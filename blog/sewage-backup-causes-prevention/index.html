Preventing Sewage Backups in Indianapolis, IN

Home / Blog / Sewage Backup Causes and Prevention in Indianapolis, IN

A sewage backup is one of the most unpleasant emergencies a homeowner can face. Once raw sewage gets into living areas, it is an immediate health hazard, it damages property extensively, and the professional cleanup it requires can run thousands of dollars. Learn the reasons these backups happen, act on a few preventive measures, and you cut the odds considerably. Indianapolis Restoration wrote this guide to walk Indianapolis, IN owners through the usual causes and some practical ways to keep a property protected.

Why Sewage Backups Happen

When something keeps wastewater from draining to the city sewer or your septic tank as designed, it comes back into the building. Most cases trace back to the following: sewer pipes that have aged or collapsed, roots from trees intruding into the pipe, grease, hair, and debris clogging the line, heavy rainfall overloading the municipal sewers, a combined sewer overflow event, or improper items going down the toilet, including excessive paper, wipes, and feminine products.

Tree Root Intrusion

In Indianapolis, many blocked sewer lines turn out to be the work of tree roots. Because roots grow toward water, a pipe that always carries it draws them in. They work their way in at cracks, joints, and connection points, then slowly fill the inside of the pipe until it blocks and backs up. Older sewer lines made of clay tile are especially easy for roots to invade. Schedule a sewer camera inspection every 2 to 3 years so root trouble is found ahead of any backup.

How to Prevent a Sewage Backup

Protecting your Indianapolis home from sewage backups involves several preventive measures: install a backwater valve on your sewer connection to prevent municipal system backflows. Never pour grease or cooking oil down drains. Toilet paper is the only thing that belongs in the toilet, so no wipes, no feminine products, and nothing else. Routine inspection and cleaning of your sewer line should be on the calendar. A drain that is running slow needs prompt attention, or it may block completely. In an emergency someone will need the sewer cleanout, so learn where yours sits. Think about adding a sewage backup endorsement to your homeowner's policy, or buying sewer line insurance.

Sewage Came Up? Do This First

Cleaning up a sewage backup on your own is something you should never try. It carries viruses, bacteria, parasites, and other dangerous pathogens capable of making you seriously ill.
